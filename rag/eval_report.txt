Question 3: Why is the gate of a MOSFET insulated?
Answer: The gate of a MOSFET is insulated from the substrate by a layer of silicon dioxide, which behaves as insulation (Excerpt 2). This insulation is necessary for the MOSFET to function properly, allowing the gate contact to control the conductance between the drain and source without being directly connected to the substrate (Excerpt 3). The gate is insulated but can still induce a conducting channel between the drain and source due to electrostatic attraction.
Reference: Because the entire surface of the substrate is coated with silicon dioxide insulation, and the gate contact is placed on that insulated layer.

Question 4: How does a MOSFET operate?
Answer: A MOSFET operates by having a gate voltage control the flow of current between the drain and source terminals. As explained in Excerpt 4 (5:14–6:45), when a gate voltage is applied, a conducting channel is formed between the drain and source due to electrostatic attraction. The device will behave as non-conducting or in off-mode below a certain threshold voltage, but when the gate voltage exceeds this threshold, the device will be in conducting or on mode, as mentioned in Excerpt 3 (6:11–7:09). The width of the channel and the conductance between drain and source increase with the applied voltage.
Reference: A MOSFET operates by using gate voltage to induce a conducting channel between the drain and source; current flows only when the gate voltage exceeds the threshold voltage.
